How I approach a product problem

Make the decisions visible.

I don't treat product work as a checklist. I focus on the decisions that can change the outcome: what I noticed, what I questioned, what I chose to test, what I deliberately left out and what I would measure next.

Alexa+One idea · seven decisions
01

Notice

Voice is fast and natural, but important information remains invisible.

Current interactionAsk → hear → act
02

Question

What if the assistant could show what it understands without making voice less effortless?

03

Investigate

Look at the moments where audio alone is weak: complex information, family learning, context and engagement.

04

Define

The opportunity is not to replace voice. It is to give voice a visual layer when that layer adds value.

05

Explore

A visual, expressive companion can appear when context benefits from it, while remaining quiet when it does not.

06

Prioritize

Start with a focused MVP: visual responses, contextual cards, simple expressive presence and core household interactions.

Deliberately outAdvanced spatial computing and other future-state ideas wait for evidence.
07

Measure & learn

Test whether people understand responses better, complete tasks faster, engage more and return more often.

The product changes.
The questions remain.

Beyond technology

As a national-level netball player and school sports captain, I learned that leadership is often less about having the strongest individual player and more about understanding the team you actually have.

When several of our strongest players moved into a higher age group, I had to give younger players unfamiliar responsibilities. Rather than reproducing the old strategy, I redistributed roles around their strengths and focused on confidence as much as execution.

That lesson still shapes how I approach product problems: understand the constraints, work with the people and resources that actually exist, and adapt the strategy without losing sight of the outcome.
